Once the claim is open, the car has its own process: a shop, an adjuster, an estimate and a queue. Knowing how the pieces fit is most of what keeps a repair from dragging for weeks.

## You choose the shop

An insurer can recommend a shop from its network, and you are free to use it or not. What matters more than the logo on the door:

- **Ask what parts they will use.** New original, aftermarket or used parts change both the price and the result.
- **Ask about the warranty** on the work, and get it in writing.
- **Ask for a written, itemized estimate.** A line-item estimate is what makes a disagreement solvable later.

## The estimate is a starting point

The first estimate covers the visible damage. When the shop takes the bumper off and finds more, they file a **supplement** with the insurer. This is normal — it is also the most common place a repair stalls, because the shop cannot continue until the supplement is approved and nobody is chasing that approval.

## When they call it a total loss

If the repair cost approaches what the car is worth, the insurer declares a total loss and pays the vehicle's **actual cash value** instead of fixing it. In practice that value is negotiable: comparable local listings, recent maintenance, low mileage and new tires are all worth raising. Ask for the valuation report behind the number.

Two things people miss here: what happens to the **loan balance** if you owe more than the payout (that is what gap coverage is for), and the deadline to **retrieve your belongings** from a car that is about to be sold at auction.

## While you have no car

Ask early, not late:

- **Rental**: who pays, from what date, and for how many days
- **Loss of use**, if there is no rental but you are without a vehicle
- **Storage**, which accumulates daily while everyone decides

## Keeping it moving

The repair and the payout run on different tracks, with different people, and neither waits for the other. Somebody has to call the adjuster the day the supplement is filed, and call the shop the day it is approved.
