The days right after a crash decide how the rest of the claim goes, and they are also the days when you are least able to think about paperwork. Here is the short version, in order.

## At the scene

- **Call the police** and get the report number. In Utah a report matters for any accident with injuries or meaningful damage, and afterwards it is the document every insurer asks for.
- **Photograph everything**: both vehicles from several angles, the plates, the position on the road, the signs and traffic lights, and the damage close up.
- **Get the other driver's information**: name, phone, insurance company and policy number.
- **Take names of witnesses.** Two minutes now, impossible to recover a week later.
- **Do not discuss fault.** Not with the other driver, not with their insurer. "I'm fine" and "it was my fault" are the two sentences people regret most, and they are usually said out of politeness or adrenaline.

## The same day or the next

- **Report the accident to your own insurer.** Most policies require prompt notice, and reporting it is not the same as filing against your own coverage.
- **Do not accept anything on the first call.** The other driver's adjuster may reach out quickly, sometimes with a number attached. That number is an opening offer, not a valuation.
- **Be careful with recorded statements.** You are generally not obligated to give the other side a recorded statement on the spot, and there is nothing wrong with saying you will get back to them.
- **Get checked out** if anything hurts. Neck and back pain often shows up a day or two later, once the adrenaline drops.

## The first week

- **Keep every piece of paper** in one place: the report number, the claim numbers, the tow and storage receipts, the rental agreement.
- **Ask about the rental early**, not late: who pays, from when, and for how many days.
- **Watch the storage clock.** A car sitting at a tow yard racks up daily fees that nobody warns you about until the bill arrives.
- **Get your own repair estimate.** One estimate is one shop's read of the visible damage; it is normal for the real number to change once the bumper comes off.

## What usually goes wrong

Three things, over and over: the claim sits because nobody chases the adjuster; the first offer is accepted because it arrives when you are tired; and the repair drags because the shop and the insurer are waiting on each other.
